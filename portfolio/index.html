PORTFOLIO / CONCEPT CASE STUDIES

Nine concepts. Nine design challenges.

This collection explores how different industries can use design, content and interaction to communicate more clearly. Every project is a self-initiated fictional brand concept, created as a working front-end demonstration rather than commissioned client work.

These websites are design and development demonstrations. The brands, products and commercial situations are fictional; no commissioned work, client results, sales, bookings or live transactions are claimed.

Motion design / Home audio

SONVIA

The brief

SONVIA is a self-initiated fictional home-audio concept exploring a question specific to web design: how can an interface visually communicate something that cannot be seen? The goal was to make the idea of sound engaging through motion and interaction without playing audio or inventing technical performance specifications.

Design direction

A graphite and muted teal visual palette, atmospheric architecture and understated product imagery establish a quiet technology identity. The page moves from a product introduction into an immersive visual story about a pulse developing into resonance and space, then returns to the physical object and its setting.

Working implementation

A scroll-linked Canvas visualization builds a changing sound-field metaphor from projected geometry, layered filaments and moving pulses. The visitor controls the progression through normal scrolling rather than a timed or locked sequence. A separate listening-mode panel offers Focus, Immersive and Night, updating the illustrative field and accompanying descriptions. Keyboard controls, motion pausing and reduced-motion handling are included.

Takeaway

Motion is most compelling when it explains an idea. By linking movement to the visitor’s progress and choices, an abstract concept becomes an interaction with a clear purpose rather than decorative animation.

Product experience / Fragrance

VELLUNE

The brief

VELLUNE is a self-initiated fictional fragrance house designed as a material and interaction study. The challenge was to make an imagined amber, cedarwood and musk composition feel tangible through the digital presentation, while offering meaningful product exploration rather than a static image alone.

Design direction

A restrained palette of warm amber, cream and muted metallic tones creates a calm luxury identity. Editorial typography and measured spacing give the product room to breathe. The composition is presented as three imagined fragrance notes, connecting the bottle appearance to the wider visual narrative.

Working implementation

The hero bottle is built from real-time Three.js geometry, including rounded glass, a fluted metal cap, layered materials and custom front and back label textures. Pointer and touch interaction rotate the actual 3D model, while scroll-driven changes add a sense of depth. The design considers reduced-motion preferences and provides a poster fallback if the interactive renderer is unavailable.

Takeaway

A product viewer becomes valuable when material, form and interaction reinforce the same story. Smooth, purposeful exploration can communicate an object’s character without requiring a visitor to navigate away from the main experience.

Brand experience / Beverage

VOLT RUSH

The brief

This self-initiated fictional beverage concept explores how a new energy-drink identity could introduce its personality and flavour range on one focused landing page. The aim was to communicate the range clearly while creating a memorable campaign experience, rather than building an online checkout or making claims about the product.

Design direction

Oversized typography, electric blue, charged lime and orange accents give the page a visual rhythm that matches the imagined brand. Cinematic product imagery establishes the focal point, while the three flavour directions share a coherent composition but retain their own colour identities.

Working implementation

The responsive page combines a campaign hero, three flavour cards and an interactive flavour spotlight. Selecting Citrus Current, Blue Surge or Solar Charge changes the highlighted label, description and accent colour, with the active button state exposed to assistive technology. A subtle pointer-led hero movement is limited to suitable desktop devices and respects reduced-motion preferences.

Takeaway

A memorable campaign can remain useful when interaction helps visitors understand the range. The most expressive visual elements work best when supported by clear hierarchy and predictable controls.

Editorial design / Fashion

FIELDNOTE

The brief

FIELDNOTE is a self-initiated fictional fashion homepage designed to explore a considered alternative to crowded clothing storefronts. Instead of presenting a dense catalogue immediately, the concept introduces the tone of a seasonal collection and gives visitors a straightforward route to discover its three categories.

Design direction

The design uses warm neutrals, relaxed photography, restrained text and generous space to create an editorial rhythm. The arrangement gives fabrics and silhouettes room to establish the identity, while the typography supports the imagery without fighting for attention. The collection is framed around everyday outerwear, refined essentials and finishing pieces.

Working implementation

The page uses responsive image-led sections, native anchor navigation and a structured collection layout. Its typography, imagery and spacing adapt to smaller screens without changing the underlying reading order. Lightweight section reveals are enabled when suitable, with reduced-motion preferences respected and the content remaining available without animation.

Takeaway

The exercise reinforces that editorial commerce does not need to be visually busy to be engaging. A strong image hierarchy and clear information architecture can make a concept feel premium without sacrificing usability.

Destination design / Hospitality

ASTER HOUSE

The brief

ASTER HOUSE is a self-initiated fictional hospitality concept about a quiet desert retreat. The design challenge was to communicate a sense of place and help a prospective visitor imagine the experience before asking them to explore the spaces. It is a visual prototype, not an accommodation provider or booking system.

Design direction

Warm sandstone tones, architectural photography, generous negative space and restrained editorial typography establish a calm pace. The hero introduces the retreat through a single visual story, followed by sections that move from the overall atmosphere to individual imagined spaces: a courtyard room, terrace suite and desert evenings.

Working implementation

Responsive layouts guide visitors through the experience and retreat sections using native navigation links. Optimised imagery and clear text hierarchy retain the narrative on narrow screens. Lightweight scroll reveals add a sense of arrival when motion is enabled, while the page remains readable without them.

Takeaway

Hospitality design is as much about helping somebody picture an experience as presenting information. Deliberate pacing, practical navigation and consistent imagery can communicate a distinct destination without inventing reservations or commercial functionality.

Product campaign / Footwear

STRIDE

The brief

STRIDE is a self-initiated fictional athletic footwear campaign designed around the idea of movement. The challenge was to communicate an energetic identity and present three different contexts for footwear without creating a complex storefront or relying on unsupported product-performance claims.

Design direction

An immersive, orange-lit track visual anchors the hero, paired with assertive typography and a high-contrast graphic language. The page transitions into three clear activity directions: Run, Train and Explore. Each section uses its own setting while retaining the same visual system, so the campaign remains coherent rather than feeling like unrelated product advertisements.

Working implementation

The responsive homepage uses an original hero image, activity-led cards, straightforward anchor navigation and lightweight reveal effects. On suitable desktop devices, pointer movement gives the hero photography a subtle spatial response; touch layouts remain stable and reduced-motion preferences are respected. This is an image-led campaign, not an interactive 3D shoe viewer or checkout experience.

Takeaway

The most effective movement in a sportswear interface is intentional. Energetic art direction can establish the brand while restrained interaction keeps the collection accessible and easy to explore.

Digital experience / Technology

GOODWORK

The brief

GOODWORK is a self-initiated fictional technology-services website exploring how a digital provider might explain complex capabilities in a clear, approachable way. The page focuses on three connected areas: web experiences, digital workflows and data insight, without presenting fictional customer projects as completed work.

Design direction

A structured visual system, direct headlines and restrained colour palette turn a broad set of services into an understandable narrative. The opening section introduces the problem of complexity; the remaining sections show how discovery, design, development and validation fit together. Service descriptions are concise and oriented around the types of needs a visitor may recognise.

Working implementation

The responsive landing page combines a process illustration, service cards and a clearly ordered process section. Navigation moves directly to the relevant content, while lightweight entrance effects support the flow without hiding information from visitors who prefer reduced motion. The page is a static service-positioning concept, not a connected consultation or workflow-management application.

Takeaway

Technology websites benefit from plain language and an explicit information structure. Visual design should make the service story easier to understand, rather than introduce another layer of complexity.

Industrial design / Infrastructure

AXIOM NETWORKS

The brief

AXIOM NETWORKS is a self-initiated fictional infrastructure website exploring how industrial product families can be introduced through a professional, product-led digital experience. The concept brings network systems, fiber connectivity and copper cabling into one coherent catalogue introduction without claiming to supply real products.

Design direction

A white interface, restrained blue accents, clear technical typography and original generated imagery create a clean enterprise aesthetic. Three cinematic hero scenes introduce the product families, while distinct content cards and a layered network diagram translate complex infrastructure into understandable categories.

Working implementation

The hero uses three locally encoded video clips with fluid camera motion. Each clip runs to its genuine media end before the next transition, with playback controls and reduced-motion handling. A client-side product-family search filters the three categories, supplies a clear empty state and lets visitors expand contextual details. The navigation and media layout adapt to mobile screens.

Takeaway

Industrial storytelling is most effective when visual impact supports practical discovery. Real video sequencing, clear filters and accessible controls make an expressive concept feel considered rather than distracting.

Editorial commerce / Skincare

MIELA

The brief

MIELA is a self-initiated fictional skincare homepage exploring a quieter, more personal approach to beauty branding. The aim was to present a simple daily ritual in a way that encourages product discovery without introducing an artificial shopping cart or making cosmetic efficacy claims.

Design direction

The warm skin-tone palette, soft natural light, tactile product imagery and editorial typography give the concept a sense of calm and care. The page avoids visual clutter and presents the three product directions as a deliberately sequenced routine: cleanse, hydrate and moisturize. The copy focuses on texture, atmosphere and the imagined experience.

Working implementation

An accessible three-step tabbed product panel updates the image, product title, description, texture and ritual information. Image changes are preloaded before a gentle transition, with reduced-motion preferences respected. Keyboard navigation supports Arrow keys, Home and End, while mobile navigation and responsive typography preserve the intended visual hierarchy.

Takeaway

A good product experience can guide somebody through a routine without overwhelming them. Thoughtful interaction, consistent visual language and concise product information can make a small collection feel both premium and understandable.
