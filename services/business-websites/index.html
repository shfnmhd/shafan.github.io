01 / Business websites

Business websites built around real customer journeys.

A business website should make it easy for someone to understand what you do, trust the information they find, and take the next step. I design and develop sites that connect clear positioning with useful functionality, whether you are introducing a new company, refreshing an existing site, or organising a growing range of services and products.

Start with the customer, not the template.

A customer might arrive from a Google search, a referral, a QR code, or a social media profile. Before designing the pages, I look at the questions each visitor needs answered: what the business offers, who it serves, where it operates, and how to get in touch. Those questions shape the navigation, page hierarchy and calls to action.

The approach is particularly useful when a company has several service categories, technical offerings, or different customer groups. A simple homepage can introduce the business while dedicated pages explain the areas that need more detail, rather than trying to fit everything into a single screen.

What the website can include.

The exact scope depends on your business and content. I plan the page structure before development so that key information is visible and supporting material is easy to find. Typical deliverables can include:

  • Responsive homepage and dedicated service, about, contact, or product information pages.
  • Clear navigation, accessible interactive elements, and mobile-friendly layouts.
  • Enquiry forms or click-to-chat contact options, with the message handling agreed in advance.
  • A structured product or service directory, including category pages where useful.
  • Page titles, descriptions, crawlable page structure, and a basic technical SEO setup.
  • Performance optimisation for image-heavy pages and a handover explaining how the site is organised.

What I need to understand about your business.

The starting point is your brand identity, the services or products you currently offer, and the customers you want to reach. Existing brochures, photography, technical documents and a current website are valuable inputs. If a new site needs content that has not yet been written, we agree whether it should be provided by your team or included as a separate content task.

I also ask which enquiries should be encouraged, who receives them, and which contact methods are appropriate. For example, a consultant-led business may want a short discovery enquiry, while an industrial supplier may need a structured product enquiry. Getting that decision right early avoids building forms or pages that do not support your actual workflow.

From first outline to a tested release.

The work begins with a sitemap and an agreed visual direction. Once the important pages are mapped out, I develop a responsive design that can grow with the content. The next stage is implementation, including forms, navigation, and any agreed integrations. Before launch, I test the intended customer paths across screen sizes and review accessibility basics, content alignment, and missing assets.

A website can be planned around a static structure, a content management system, or a tailored application. The choice determines the hosting needs and how easily different people can edit content. Existing domains and email services are reviewed before deployment so that the scope of any changes is understood.

Scope, ownership and next steps.

Business websites do not automatically include a full customer portal, online payments, custom business software or ongoing marketing management. If you need those features, they can be considered separately, with a clear understanding of the data, integration and maintenance involved. Content ownership, editing arrangements and post-launch responsibilities should be agreed before development begins.
