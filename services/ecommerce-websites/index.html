02 / E-commerce websites

Online stores and catalogues that make products easier to choose.

People compare products differently online than they do in a physical store. They need to understand the differences quickly, find the right option, and know exactly what happens when they enquire, add an item, or place an order. I design product experiences around those choices, with the technical workflow planned alongside the visual design.

Decide what kind of online store you actually need.

Not every product website needs an online payment system. Some businesses sell directly to customers, while others use a catalogue to generate quotations or communicate technical product information. There are also stores that need both workflows, with retail purchases for some items and enquiries for larger or more specialised orders.

The first step is to agree which model fits your business. We identify the product categories, customer groups, fulfilment process and whether prices and availability can be displayed reliably. This helps prevent unnecessary checkout complexity and makes the resulting site more useful for customers and staff.

Build a catalogue customers can navigate.

Product pages should do more than look attractive. They should make distinctions between products clear, particularly when there are sizes, finishes, colours, specifications or other variants. A consistent catalogue structure also helps your team manage products without rebuilding the site every time the range changes.

  • Category structure, product lists and search or filter options appropriate to the size of the catalogue.
  • Responsive product detail pages with imagery, descriptions, variant selections and relevant supporting documents.
  • Shopping cart and checkout journeys, if direct online payment is part of the agreed scope.
  • A product enquiry or quotation flow as an alternative to checkout, where appropriate.
  • Clear handling of empty results, unavailable items, validation and other common customer states.
  • An agreed content editing or administration workflow for maintaining product information.

What your team needs to prepare.

A useful store starts with product data. I ask for names, categories, images, descriptions, specifications, variant relationships and SKU identifiers where available. If stock or price information will appear on the site, we should agree where it comes from and how often it changes. The quality and consistency of these inputs affect both the development process and the customer experience.

For a transactional store, you will also need to define delivery coverage, shipping costs or rules, returns, tax treatment, and the payment provider you intend to use. Your business is responsible for approving the relevant policies and confirming that its commercial arrangements are appropriate. I can help design the website around those agreed rules.

Choose the right technology and purchase flow.

A smaller store may be better suited to an established commerce platform. A more complex catalogue may need custom product relationships or an integration with an existing inventory system. I weigh these options against the desired buying experience, the administrative workload, the cost of maintaining the solution and the integrations you actually need.

For online payments, a hosted payment flow from an established provider can reduce the need for the website itself to handle sensitive card information. The selected provider, account setup, fees and merchant requirements remain separate decisions. Testing should cover the agreed purchase journey, including errors, cancellations, confirmation screens and recorded order status, using appropriate test facilities before live transactions are enabled.

What is included depends on the agreed scope.

Product data entry at scale, stock synchronisation, automated tax calculations, payment provider onboarding, third-party delivery connections and ongoing store operation are not automatically part of every ecommerce website. Each may require separate work depending on the systems already in use. We agree the boundaries and responsibilities before development.
